[r2_10] Blood Relations / Coded Relations
If A + B means A is father of B, A - B means A is wife of B, A x B means A is brother of B. Which of the following shows P is grandmother of Q?
  A. P - S + R + Q <-- CORRECT
  B. P - R + Q
  C. P + S + R x Q
  D. P x S - R + Q
  (He skipped this one)
Why: P - S (P is wife of S) + R (S is father of R) + Q (R is father of Q) => S is father of R, R is father of Q, so S is Q's grandfather; P is S's wife, so P is Q's grandmother.
Trick: Grandmother needs a THREE-link chain (wife of [father of [father of Q]]) — a shorter 'P - R + Q' only reaches Q's MOTHER (wife of Q's own father), not grandmother. Count the generations the chain actually spans before picking an answer.

[r2_19] Coding-Decoding / Positional Word Coding
If in a code, SUN is written as VV and STAR is written as WW, how is MOON written in the same code?
  A. QQ <-- CORRECT
  B. PP
  C. RR
  D. NN
  (He skipped this one)
Why: Rule: take the word's first letter, shift it forward by the word's LENGTH, then double it. SUN: S(19)+3=22=V -> VV. STAR: S(19)+4=23=W -> WW. MOON: M(13)+4=17=Q -> QQ.
Trick: Derive the exact rule from BOTH given examples first (first-letter shifted by word-length, then doubled) — a rule based on only one example could be coincidental; confirming it against two independent words makes it reliable.

[r2_22] Seating Arrangement / Circular Arrangement
Six people P,Q,R,S,T,U sit around a circular table facing the centre, equally spaced. P is second to the right of Q. T is second to the left of Q. R and S occupy the two remaining seats next to P and T. Who sits directly opposite Q?
  A. U <-- CORRECT
  B. R or S <-- HIS ANSWER (WRONG)
  C. P
  D. T
Why: Seat Q, then place P two seats clockwise and T two seats anticlockwise from Q — this fixes 3 of the 6 seats (Q, P, T) with one empty seat between each pair. The seat directly opposite Q (3 seats away) is the one remaining seat, occupied by U.
Trick: In a 6-seat circle, 'opposite' always means exactly 3 seats away. Fix the three named positions first — only one seat is then left for the last unplaced person.

[r2_25] Non-Verbal Reasoning / Mirror Image of Clock Time

Why: Mirror image of a clock time uses the formula: Mirror time = 11:60 minus the actual time. 11:60 - 4:20 = 7:40.

[q2_04] Geometry / Parallel Chords (reverse for radius)
Two parallel chords of lengths 16 cm and 12 cm lie on opposite sides of the centre of a circle, 14 cm apart. Find the radius of the circle.
  A. 10 cm <-- CORRECT
  B. 9 cm
  C. 12 cm
  D. 11 cm
  (He skipped this one)
Why: Let the chords' distances from the centre be d1 and d2, with d1+d2=14. From the chord-radius relation: r^2 = 8^2+d1^2 = 6^2+d2^2. Subtracting: d2^2-d1^2=28, and since d2+d1=14, (d2-d1)=28/14=2. So d1=6, d2=8. r^2 = 8^2+6^2 = 100, r = 10 cm.
Trick: With chords on OPPOSITE sides, their distances from the centre ADD to the given gap (not subtract) — set up two simultaneous equations in d1 and d2 rather than guessing a single distance for both chords.

[q2_22] Trigonometry / Ratio Identity from tanA

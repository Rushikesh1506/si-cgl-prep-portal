# Day-1 FULL: Articles 14–18 — Right to Equality (30-minute teacher lesson)

## 0. Why this lesson decides rank (2 min)

Arts 14–18 get asked **by number, not by name**. The formats are match-type, multi-statement and exception traps. These are marks a general-category candidate cannot afford to drop — toppers score full here.

Read slowly. Every line below is either a past question or the seed of one.

## 1. Where Art 14 came from (4 min)

Two ideas, welded into one sentence.

- **"Equality before law"** — British, from **Dicey**. A **negative** idea: *nobody is above the law*.
- **"Equal protection of the laws"** — American, from the **14th Amendment**. A **positive** idea: *treat equals equally* — which means **unequals may be treated differently** (tax slabs, age limits, reservation).

Art 14: *"The State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or the equal protection of the laws within the territory of India."*

**"Person"** = citizen + foreigner + **company** in appropriate cases. Contrast Arts 15 and 16, which say **"citizen"**.

**"State" (Art 12)** = the Government and Parliament of India, the Government and Legislature of each State, and all **local or other authorities**.

⚠️ **Correction to the earlier version of this lesson:** it listed "courts" flatly as State. The judiciary is State only in its **administrative and rule-making** capacity. **Judicial orders cannot be challenged as Fundamental Rights violations** (*Naresh Shridhar Mirajkar* 1967; *A.R. Antulay* 1988).

**Memory hook:** BEFORE (British, negative — nobody above) + PROTECTION (American, positive — equals get an equal shield).

## 2. The test the exam actually checks (6 min)

**Banned — class legislation.** Picking out one person or group with no real reason.

**Allowed — reasonable classification.** Two conditions, **both compulsory**:

- **(a) Intelligible differentia** — a real, visible difference between those grouped in and those left out.
- **(b) Rational nexus** — that difference must connect to **the law's object**.

Passes: age 21+ for an SI post — differentia is age, nexus is maturity and fitness.
Fails: "only candidates born on a Monday are eligible" — the differentia is perfectly intelligible, but there is **no nexus**. Most candidates forget that limb (b) can kill a rule on its own.

**Then the second blade.** *E.P. Royappa* (1974, promotion denied without reason) and *Maneka Gandhi* (1978, passport impounded without a hearing): **arbitrariness alone violates Art 14**, even where the classification looks clean. *Shayara Bano* (2017) extended this to strike down **legislation** itself — "manifest arbitrariness".

So the examiner's favourite setup is a **fair-looking rule applied arbitrarily**. The answer is still **Art 14 violation**.

## 3. Article by article (10 min)

### ART 14 — all persons
The two phrases above, subject to the exceptions in §4.

### ART 15 — citizens only, five grounds only
**Religion, Race, Caste, Sex, Place of birth.** Descent and residence are **NOT** here.

- **15(1)** binds the **State** only.
- **15(2)** — ⚠️ **the correction that matters most in this lesson. It has TWO limbs, and only the second carries the funding condition.**
  - **15(2)(a)** — access to **shops, public restaurants, hotels and places of public entertainment**. **NO State-funding condition.** Applies to **privately owned** shops and hotels. This is one of the rare Fundamental Rights that operates **horizontally** — citizen against citizen.
  - **15(2)(b)** — use of **wells, tanks, bathing ghats, roads and places of public resort** — **these** must be wholly or partly maintained out of **State funds**, or dedicated to the public.

  The earlier version of this lesson merged the two and said the funding condition applied to shops. It does not. A **private hotel** refusing service on caste grounds **violates 15(2)(a)** — and is separately an offence under the **SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act**. What is *not* covered is a **private home**, which appears in neither limb.
- **15(3)** special provision for **women and children**.
- **15(4)** SEBC/SC/ST advancement — **1st Amendment 1951**, after *Champakam Dorairajan*.
- **15(5)** private unaided institutions — **93rd Amendment 2005**, **expressly excepting minority institutions** under Art 30.
- **15(6)** EWS, up to 10% — **103rd Amendment 2019**.

### ART 16 — citizens only, public employment, seven grounds
The five of Art 15 **plus descent and residence**.

- **16(3)** a **residence requirement** for a State post may be prescribed by **Parliament alone** — not by the State legislature.
- **16(4)** reservation for backward classes; **16(4A)** promotion with consequential seniority for SC/ST (77th, 85th); **16(4B)** backlog vacancies (81st); **16(6)** EWS.

***Indra Sawhney* (Mandal, 1992, 9 judges)** — four holdings:
1. **16(4) is NOT an exception** to 16(1); it is a **facet of real equality**.
2. **50% ceiling** on backward-class reservation.
3. **Creamy layer** excluded.
4. Promotion quota disallowed — later restored by the **77th Amendment**.

⚠️ **Update — the ceiling moved.** ***Janhit Abhiyan* (2022)** upheld the 103rd Amendment and held the **50% ceiling is NOT part of the basic structure**, with **EWS sitting outside it**. "Reservation can never exceed 50%" is now a **false** statement.

### ART 17 — one line, absolute
Untouchability is **abolished**; its practice in any form is an **offence**. **No exception, no reasonable restriction.** It binds **private individuals** — horizontal. Enforced through the **Protection of Civil Rights Act 1955** and the **SC/ST (PoA) Act 1989**. The Constitution deliberately **does not define** "untouchability".

### ART 18 — titles
- No **titles** from the State, **except military and academic** distinctions.
- **18(2)** a **citizen may not accept a foreign title at all**. **18(3)** a **non-citizen holding an office of profit** under the State needs the **President's consent**. (The earlier version of this lesson blurred the two.)
- ***Balaji Raghavan* (1996)** — Bharat Ratna and the Padma awards are **awards, not titles**, so they survive Art 18, but they may **not be used as a prefix or suffix**.

## 4. Exceptions — where Art 14 does NOT reach (3 min)

- **Art 361** — the President and Governors answer to **no court** for the exercise of their office, and face no criminal proceedings while in office.
- **Art 359** — during an emergency, the right to move courts for specified FRs may be suspended by **Presidential order** — but **never for Arts 20 and 21** after the 44th Amendment.
- **Art 31C** — laws giving effect to **Art 39(b)–(c)** are shielded from Arts 14 and 19. The 42nd Amendment tried to extend this to **all** DPSP; *Minerva Mills* (1980) cut it back. ⚠️ **Update:** *Property Owners Association* (Nov 2024, 9 judges) held the **unamended 31C survives** and **narrowed Art 39(b)** — not every private resource is a "material resource of the community".
- **Art 33** — Parliament may restrict FRs for the **armed forces and forces charged with maintaining public order**. This is the police officer's own Article.
- **Arts 105 / 194** — parliamentary and legislative privileges.

Also: diplomatic and UN immunities. If an option says **"Art 14 is absolute with no exception"**, it is **false**.

## 5. Amendment spine for this lesson (2 min)

- **1st (1951)** — Art 15(4) and the Ninth Schedule.
- **32nd (1973)** — **Arts 371D and 371E for Andhra Pradesh.** See Module C; this is the Article that governs your own recruitment.
- **42nd (1976)** — "mini-Constitution": Socialist, Secular, Integrity in the Preamble; Arts 39A, 43A, 48A; Art 51A (10 duties); widened 31C.
- **44th (1978)** — armed rebellion replaces internal disturbance; Arts 20 and 21 non-suspendable; property moved out of Part III to **Art 300A**.
- **86th (2002)** — Art 21A, Art 45 reworded, Art 51A(k).
- **91st (2003)** — Council of Ministers capped at 15%.
- **93rd (2005)** — Art 15(5).
- **103rd (2019)** — EWS, Arts 15(6) and 16(6).
- **104th (2020)** — Anglo-Indian nomination ended; **Lok Sabha maximum fell from 552 to 550**.

Swap traps to drill: **91st vs 103rd**, **42nd vs 44th**, **86th vs 93rd**.

## 6. The AP connection — Art 371D (2 min)

*(This section was missing from the earlier version of this lesson.)*

Art 16(3) is not the only way geography enters public employment in this State.

- **Art 371D**, inserted by the **32nd Amendment (1973)** after the **Six-Point Formula** that ended the **Jai Andhra agitation** — during which AP was under **President's Rule in January 1973**.
- It empowers the **President**, by **order**, to organise civil posts into **local cadres** and to provide for **reservation in direct recruitment by local area**, and to constitute an **Administrative Tribunal** for the State.
- The operative instrument is the **AP Public Employment (Organisation of Local Cadres and Regulation of Direct Recruitment) Order, 1975** — "the Presidential Order".
- It **survived the 2014 bifurcation** and applies to both AP and Telangana.

**The contrast to memorise:** **Art 16(3) = Parliament, by LAW. Art 371D = the President, by ORDER.** Examiners put both in the same set of options.

## 7. Case stories — remember people, not paragraphs (5 min)

- ***Royappa* (1974)** — a Tamil Nadu officer denied promotion without reason. Bhagwati J: equality is dynamic, it cannot be cribbed or cabined; **arbitrariness is its sworn enemy**. One line: *no reason = no equality.*
- ***Maneka Gandhi* (1978)** — passport impounded "in the public interest", no hearing. Procedure under Art 21 must be **fair, just and reasonable**, else Arts 14 and 21 both fail. One line: *the process must be just, not merely exist.*
- ***Indra Sawhney* (1992, 9 judges)** — 16(4) is a **method of equality**, 50% ceiling, creamy layer out. One line: *quota is equality in action, capped at half — except EWS, after 2022.*
- ***Minerva Mills* (1980)** — the 42nd's all-DPSP shield cut back to **39(b)–(c)**; the **FR–DPSP balance is itself basic structure**. One line: *the shield shrank to two clauses.*
- ***Balaji Raghavan* (1996)** — national awards are **not titles**; **no prefix**. One line: *award yes, title no.*
- ***Janhit Abhiyan* (2022)** — EWS upheld; the 50% ceiling is **not** basic structure. One line: *the ceiling bent for EWS.*

## 8. Fourteen one-line killers — night sheet (3 min)

1. **14 = person; 15 and 16 = citizen.**
2. **15 has exactly 5 grounds** (RRCSP).
3. **Descent and residence appear only in Art 16.**
4. **Residence requirement: Parliament alone (16(3)). Local cadres: the President (371D).**
5. **15(2)(a) has NO State-funds condition and binds private shops and hotels.** Only 15(2)(b) needs State funds.
6. **Horizontal FRs: 15(2), 17, 23, 24.**
7. **15(5) skips minority institutions.**
8. **The 50% ceiling binds 16(4), not EWS** (Janhit Abhiyan 2022).
9. **Art 17 is absolute.**
10. **Art 18 allows military and academic distinctions; a citizen cannot take a foreign title at all.**
11. **Exceptions: 361, 359, 31C, 33, 105/194.**
12. **31C = 39(b)–(c) only** — and *Property Owners Association* 2024 narrowed 39(b).
13. **EWS = 103rd, not 91st.**
14. **The judiciary is State only in its administrative role.**

### The five-step drill for any Arts 14–18 question

1. **Person or citizen?** A foreigner or company in the stem → only Art 14 survives; kill every 15/16 option.
2. **Count the grounds.** Descent or residence in the stem → **Art 16**, never Art 15.
3. **Spot "State funds" next to shops or hotels** → the statement is **false**.
4. **"Reservation is an exception"** → **false** (Indra Sawhney). **"Never exceeds 50%"** → **false** (Janhit Abhiyan).
5. **Match-type:** fix **Art 17** first (always untouchability), then **Art 14** (always the "equality before law" phrase). The remaining two fall into place.

**Blind drills:** write `14-PERSON 15-CIT 16-CIT 17-ABS 18-NOTITLE` five times. Recite **RRCSP + descent/residence only in 16**. Recite **361–359–31C–33–105** as "crown, crisis, cause, corps, chamber". Recite **16(3) Parliament / 371D President**.

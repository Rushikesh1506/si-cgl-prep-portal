# Fundamental Duties - Article 51A (Part IVA)
### Complete Chapter for AP SI Aspirants | Zero Background Friendly

> One-line memory: **Rights = what country gives you. Duties = what you give country.**

If Fundamental Rights (Part III) are your *shield*, Fundamental Duties (Part IVA) are your *responsibility*. AP SI exam loves this chapter because it is small but full of traps: dates, numbers, amendments, and "which duty is which".

---

## 1. Origin Story: Where Did Duties Come From?

**1. Not in the original Constitution (1950):**
When the Constitution started on 26 January 1950, it had Fundamental Rights but NO Fundamental Duties. The makers felt a new free nation needed rights first.

**2. Swaran Singh Committee, 1976:**
During the Emergency (1975-77), the government appointed the **Swaran Singh Committee** in 1976 to suggest duties. The Committee recommended **8 duties**.

**3. 42nd Amendment Act, 1976:**
Parliament accepted the idea but added **10 duties** through the **42nd Constitutional Amendment, 1976**. This amendment is called the **Mini-Constitution** because it made many big changes. It inserted new **Part IVA** containing only one Article: **Article 51A**.

**4. Inspiration - USSR:**
The idea of written Fundamental Duties was borrowed from the **Constitution of the USSR (Soviet Union)**. No other major democracy at that time had such a list.

**5. 11th Duty - 86th Amendment, 2002:**
Originally 10 duties (a to j). In 2002, the **86th Amendment** added duty **(k)** about children's education (age 6-14). So today: **11 duties, Article 51A (a) to (k).**

**Exam traps:**
- Committee = Swaran Singh, 1976. Amendment = 42nd, 1976.
- Committee suggested 8, Amendment added 10.
- Part = IVA, Article = 51A only.
- USSR inspiration, not USA/UK.
- 11th duty = 86th Amendment, 2002 (not 42nd).

## 2. Why Duties Alongside Rights?

Teacher logic: Rights without duties become selfishness.

1. **Balance:** If you have Right to Speech, you also have Duty to protect unity (51A-c) and harmony (51A-e). You cannot use speech to break the country.
2. **Democracy needs citizens:** Constitution does not run on police alone. It runs when citizens respect flag, protect environment, pay attention to science, protect public property.
3. **For police:** A citizen who knows only rights will argue at every checkpoint. A citizen who knows duties will cooperate for national service, harmony, non-violence.

Supreme Court has said: Rights and Duties are two sides of same coin. While interpreting laws, courts keep duties in mind.

## 3. Duty (a): Respect Constitution, Flag, Anthem

**1. What does it say?**
To abide by the Constitution and respect its ideals and institutions, the National Flag and the National Anthem.

**2. Simple meaning**
Follow the Constitution, respect Parliament, judiciary, Election Commission, and always honour the Flag and Anthem. Respect means both obey and honour.

**3. Why it exists**
Free India was new in 1950. Without common symbols and respect for institutions, unity would break. This duty creates a shared national identity.

**4. Real-life example**
Standing respectfully when Jana Gana Mana plays in school or cinema, and not throwing a torn paper flag on the road after 15th August.

**5. AP SI relevance**
During Republic Day / Independence Day bandobast, SI ensures anthem protocol, stops vehicles respectfully, prevents flag insult, and books those who burn or dishonour the flag.

**6. Related laws**
Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971 - punishes burning, mutilating or dishonouring flag, Constitution and anthem. Flag Code of India, 2002 - rules for correct display.

**7. Exam points**
Article 51A(a). Part IVA. Added by 42nd Amendment 1976 on Swaran Singh Committee 1976 recommendation. Covers ideals + institutions + flag + anthem, not flag alone.

**8. Common trap + sample MCQ**
Trap: Institutions + ideals are also in (a), not only flag/anthem.
Q1. Which Article contains Fundamental Duties? A) 51A B) 51 C) 50 D) 52. **Ans: A**
Q2. Insult to National Flag is punishable under? A) 1971 Act B) 1986 Act C) 1972 Act D) 2009 Act. **Ans: A**

## 4. Duty (b): Cherish Freedom Struggle Ideals

**1. What does it say?**
To cherish and follow the noble ideals which inspired our national struggle for freedom.

**2. Simple meaning**
Remember and live by freedom fighters' values - non-violence, unity, sacrifice, Swaraj. Not just photos on walls, but behaviour in life.

**3. Why it exists**
Freedom came from sacrifice. The makers feared the next generation would forget those values, so this duty links past struggle to present conduct.

**4. Real-life example**
Remembering Gandhi's non-violence, Bhagat Singh's courage and Sarojini Naidu's unity message in daily conduct, and teaching children why freedom matters.

**5. AP SI relevance**
During school visits and community policing, SI quotes freedom ideals to motivate youth against drugs, rowdyism and violence.

**6. Related laws**
No single punishing law. Protected through national martyrs' memorials, official celebrations and history education policy.

**7. Exam points**
Article 51A(b). Original 10 duties added by 42nd Amendment 1976 after Swaran Singh Committee 1976. Keywords: cherish and follow, noble ideals.

**8. Common trap + sample MCQ**
Trap: (b) is about past ideals, (c) is about present unity. Do not mix. (b) = freedom fighters' values.
Q1. Duty (b) relates to? A) Freedom struggle ideals B) Environment C) Science D) Property. **Ans: A**
Q2. Which struggle inspired 51A(b)? A) National freedom struggle B) French Revolution C) Russian Revolution D) UN movement. **Ans: A**

## 5. Duty (c): Protect Sovereignty, Unity, Integrity

**1. What does it say?**
To uphold and protect the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India.

**2. Simple meaning**
Sovereignty = no outside control, Unity = people stay together, Integrity = territory stays whole. Keep the country one and independent.

**3. Why it exists**
India is diverse and faced separatism and wars of 1962, 1965 and 1971 before 1976. Without unity, rights mean nothing.

**4. Real-life example**
Not sharing fake separatist propaganda on WhatsApp and not supporting enemy-country slogans.

**5. AP SI relevance**
Most direct SI duty. Intelligence gathering on anti-national activity, action against secessionist pamphlets and slogans, and bandobast in sensitive communal situations.

**6. Related laws**
UAPA 1967, BNS provisions on sovereignty-related offences (now BNS 152), Official Secrets Act 1923.

**7. Exam points**
Article 51A(c). Original 10 in 1976, total now 10+1 = 11 after 86th Amendment 2002. Exact order asked: sovereignty, unity, integrity.

**8. Common trap + sample MCQ**
Trap: Order is sovereignty, unity, integrity. Options often jumble it.
Q1. 51A(c) protects? A) Sovereignty, unity, integrity B) Liberty, equality C) Property, violence D) Culture only. **Ans: A**
Q2. An SI stopping separatist slogans is enforcing spirit of? A) 51A(c) B) 51A(g) C) 51A(h) D) 51A(j). **Ans: A**

## 6. Duty (d): Defend Country, National Service

**1. What does it say?**
To defend the country and render national service when called upon to do so.

**2. Simple meaning**
Be ready to help the nation in war, disaster or emergency when the government calls you. It is conditional duty, not daily compulsory army service.

**3. Why it exists**
Army and police alone cannot defend a country of 140 crore in war or disaster. Citizens are the reserve strength.

**4. Real-life example**
Joining NCC or NSS, donating blood during war, volunteering in floods, and joining civil defence mock drills.

**5. AP SI relevance**
SI is the caller of national service - mobilising volunteers during cyclones in coastal AP, floods in Godavari districts, and law-and-order emergencies.

**6. Related laws**
No conscription law in India. Service is voluntary except emergency requisition under Civil Defence Act 1968 and Disaster Management Act 2005.

**7. Exam points**
Article 51A(d). Part of original 10 from 42nd Amendment 1976. Key phrase: when called upon to do so.

**8. Common trap + sample MCQ**
Trap: India has NO compulsory military service. Duty activates only on demand.
Q1. 51A(d) says? A) Defend country + national service when called B) Free education C) Scientific temper D) Protect property. **Ans: A**
Q2. Which is example of 51A(d)? A) Civil defence volunteering in flood B) Littering C) Breaking curfew D) Burning bus. **Ans: A**

## 7. Duty (e): Harmony, Brotherhood, Dignity of Women

**1. What does it say?**
To promote harmony and the spirit of common brotherhood amongst all the people of India transcending religious, linguistic and regional or sectional diversities, and to renounce practices derogatory to the dignity of women.

**2. Simple meaning**
Live like brothers and sisters beyond religion, language and region, and give up customs that insult women like dowry and harassment.

**3. Why it exists**
Partition trauma, riots and caste-religion divide plus continued discrimination against women made the makers put unity and gender respect in one duty.

**4. Real-life example**
Celebrating festivals together, stopping hate forwards, and opposing dowry, eve-teasing and witch-branding.

**5. AP SI relevance**
Core SI work - preventing communal clash in sensitive areas, taking prompt FIR in crimes against women, SHE Teams and Mahila police work, and counselling against dowry harassment.

**6. Related laws**
Dowry Prohibition Act 1961, Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act 2005, BNS provisions on hurting religious harmony, PoSH Act 2013.

**7. Exam points**
Article 51A(e). One of original 10 from 1976. It has two parts in one duty: harmony plus renounce anti-women practices.

**8. Common trap + sample MCQ**
Trap: Words transcending religious, linguistic and regional or sectional diversities belong only to (e).
Q1. Renouncing practices against dignity of women is in? A) 51A(e) B) 51A(a) C) 51A(g) D) 51A(i). **Ans: A**
Q2. SI conducting flag march to stop Hindu-Muslim clash upholds? A) 51A(e) B) 51A(f) C) 51A(h) D) 51A(k). **Ans: A**

## 8. Duty (f): Preserve Composite Culture

**1. What does it say?**
To value and preserve the rich heritage of our composite culture.

**2. Simple meaning**
Protect India's mixed heritage - temples, mosques, churches, Kuchipudi, manuscripts and forts. Composite means Hindu-Muslim-Sikh-Christian-tribal together, not one community alone.

**3. Why it exists**
India's strength is its mixed culture. Without conscious preservation, monuments, arts and traditions would decay or be divided by communal feelings.

**4. Real-life example**
Not defacing Hampi or Lepakshi monuments, respecting all festivals, and learning Telugu heritage along with other cultures.

**5. AP SI relevance**
Protecting heritage sites from vandalism and idol theft, controlling crowds during Brahmotsavam or Urs without hurting sentiments, and acting against smuggling of antiquities.

**6. Related laws**
Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act 1958, Antiquities and Art Treasures Act 1972.

**7. Exam points**
Article 51A(f). One of original 10 from 42nd Amendment 1976. Keyword composite culture appears only here.

**8. Common trap + sample MCQ**
Trap: (f) = culture and monuments, (g) = nature and environment. Do not swap.
Q1. 51A(f) asks to preserve? A) Composite culture heritage B) Forests C) Scientific temper D) Public property. **Ans: A**
Q2. Vandalising a protected temple wall violates spirit of? A) 51A(f) B) 51A(d) C) 51A(k) D) 51A(b). **Ans: A**

## 9. Duty (g): Environment, Forests, Animals

**1. What does it say?**
To protect and improve the natural environment including forests, lakes, rivers and wildlife, and to have compassion for living creatures.

**2. Simple meaning**
Keep air, water, forests and animals safe, plant trees, stop pollution, and be kind to animals.

**3. Why it exists**
1970s saw Chipko movement, wildlife decline and pollution. Stockholm Conference 1972 pushed India to add environment in the Constitution. Same 42nd Amendment added DPSP Article 48A for the State.

**4. Real-life example**
Not dumping waste in Hussain Sagar or Krishna river, planting trees, and not showing cruelty to stray dogs or cattle.

**5. AP SI relevance**
Enforcing action against sand-mining mafia, red-sanders smuggling in Chittoor-Nellore belt, illegal wildlife trade, and noise or pollution violations during festivals.

**6. Related laws**
Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972, Forest (Conservation) Act 1980, Environment (Protection) Act 1986, Prevention of Cruelty to Animals Act 1960.

**7. Exam points**
Article 51A(g). Original 10 duties of 1976. Pair with Article 48A: 48A = State duty, 51A(g) = citizen duty, both by 42nd Amendment.

**8. Common trap + sample MCQ**
Trap: Only duty that mentions forests, lakes, rivers, wildlife plus compassion.
Q1. Compassion for living creatures is in? A) 51A(g) B) 51A(e) C) 51A(i) D) 51A(j). **Ans: A**
Q2. Citizen + State environment provisions added by? A) 42nd Amendment (48A + 51A-g) B) 86th Amendment C) 44th Amendment D) 52nd Amendment. **Ans: A**

## 10. Duty (h): Scientific Temper, Humanism, Inquiry

**1. What does it say?**
To develop the scientific temper, humanism and the spirit of inquiry and reform.

**2. Simple meaning**
Think with reason, be kind to humans, ask questions and reform bad customs. Fight superstition with science.

**3. Why it exists**
Nehru called scientific temper essential to fight superstition - witchcraft, blind faith and caste rigidity. Reform society with reason.

**4. Real-life example**
Not believing WhatsApp miracle cures, supporting vaccination, and questioning dowry or blind customs.

**5. AP SI relevance**
Acting against fake babas, black-magic fraud and human-sacrifice rumours, and spreading awareness against mob lynching over child-lifting rumours on social media.

**6. Related laws**
No direct punishment for lack of temper, but used with Drugs and Magic Remedies Act 1954 and BNS cheating provisions against superstition fraud.

**7. Exam points**
Article 51A(h). Original 10 of 1976. Only duty with three ideas: science + humanism + inquiry and reform.

**8. Common trap + sample MCQ**
Trap: Words scientific temper = only (h). Often confused with excellence in (j).
Q1. Scientific temper, humanism, inquiry is? A) 51A(h) B) 51A(j) C) 51A(c) D) 51A(a). **Ans: A**
Q2. SI arresting a fraud baba for magic cure cheating upholds spirit of? A) 51A(h) B) 51A(f) C) 51A(d) D) 51A(k). **Ans: A**

---

## 11. Duty (i): Public Property, Non-Violence

**1. What does it say?**
To safeguard public property and to abjure violence.

**2. Simple meaning**
Protect buses, trains, stations and government property, and give up violence. Abjure means renounce or avoid. Protest peacefully.

**3. Why it exists**
Bandhs and riots destroy public property for which the public pays. Freedom struggle taught non-violence, and free India must keep it.

**4. Real-life example**
Not scratching RTC bus seats, not breaking signals, and protesting without stone-pelting or arson.

**5. AP SI relevance**
Direct daily work - preventing damage during agitations, videographing arson, booking under Damage to Public Property Act, and using force only as per law to stop violence.

**6. Related laws**
Prevention of Damage to Public Property Act 1984 and Railways Act provisions.

**7. Exam points**
Article 51A(i). One of original 10 of 1976. Shortest duty wording, easy to spot in options.

**8. Common trap + sample MCQ**
Trap: Shortest duty, easiest to spot - public property plus abjure violence.
Q1. 51A(i) deals with? A) Public property + non-violence B) Education C) Environment D) Flag. **Ans: A**
Q2. Burning a police bus in protest violates? A) 51A(i) B) 51A(b) C) 51A(k) D) 51A(e) second part only. **Ans: A**

## 12. Duty (j): Excellence

**1. What does it say?**
To strive towards excellence in all spheres of individual and collective activity so that the nation constantly rises to higher levels of endeavour and achievement.

**2. Simple meaning**
Do your best work as a person and as a team - best farmer, teacher, police officer or athlete - so the nation moves higher.

**3. Why it exists**
After freedom, nation-building needed quality, not just minimum work. Excellence in every field builds a strong country.

**4. Real-life example**
A student studying sincerely, an athlete training hard, and a worker doing quality work instead of time-pass work.

**5. AP SI relevance**
Best example for you - excellent investigation with proper FIR, scene protection and chargesheet on time, plus fitness, discipline and honest duty.

**6. Related laws**
No specific punishing law. Implemented through awards like Padma and Arjuna, service rules and education policy.

**7. Exam points**
Article 51A(j). One of original 10 of 1976. Longest wording with phrase higher levels of endeavour and achievement.

**8. Common trap + sample MCQ**
Trap: (j) is general excellence, (k) is specific schooling for 6-14. Do not mix.
Q1. Strive towards excellence is? A) 51A(j) B) 51A(h) C) 51A(g) D) 51A(e). **Ans: A**
Q2. Which phrase belongs to 51A(j)? A) Higher levels of endeavour and achievement B) Composite culture C) Scientific temper D) Sovereignty. **Ans: A**

## 13. Duty (k): Education for Children 6-14

**1. What does it say?**
A parent or guardian must provide opportunities for education to his child or ward between the age of six and fourteen years.

**2. Simple meaning**
Every parent must send children aged 6 to 14 to school, not to labour work. It is the only duty addressed to parents, not all citizens.

**3. Why it exists**
Added by 86th Amendment 2002 which also made education a Fundamental Right under Article 21A for 6-14. Duty on parents matches the right of the child.

**4. Real-life example**
Enrolling a child in school, ensuring daily attendance, and not sending the child to work in shops, fields or houses.

**5. AP SI relevance**
Enforcing action against child labour, rescuing dropouts and begging children, and coordinating with schools and Child Welfare Committee in Operation Muskaan-type drives.

**6. Related laws**
Article 21A (Right to Education 6-14), Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education (RTE) Act 2009, Child Labour (Prohibition) Act 1986 as amended in 2016.

**7. Exam points**
Article 51A(k). Only duty added later by 86th Amendment 2002, not by 42nd Amendment 1976. Total is now 10+1 = 11. Age 6-14 only.

**8. Common trap + sample MCQ**
Trap: Exam asks which duty was added later = (k). Age trap: 6-14, not 0-6 or 14-18.
Q1. 51A(k) was added by? A) 86th Amendment 2002 B) 42nd Amendment 1976 C) 44th Amendment 1978 D) 52nd Amendment 1985. **Ans: A**
Q2. 51A(k) age group? A) 6-14 years B) 0-6 years C) 14-18 years D) 5-10 years. **Ans: A**

## 14. Are Duties Enforceable? Verma Committee + 2024 Position

**Simple teacher answer: No direct case in court.**

1. **Non-justiciable:** Like DPSP, you cannot go to court saying "he violated his duty, punish him". No writ for duties alone.
2. **But not useless:** Parliament *can* make laws to give teeth to duties. Example: 1971 Honour Act gives teeth to 51A(a), 1984 Public Property Act gives teeth to 51A(i), RTE Act 2009 gives teeth to 51A(k). Courts also use duties to interpret laws - e.g., upholding environment restrictions citing 51A(g).
3. **Verma Committee (1998-1999):** Justice J.S. Verma Committee on operationalisation of Fundamental Duties identified existing legal provisions (around 20+ statutes) that already implement duties and suggested awareness programmes in schools, not new punishments for every duty.
4. **2024 plea outcome:** Supreme Court was asked to command/enforce duties by orders. Court declined mandamus, observing duties are to be *nurtured and promoted through education and awareness, not commanded by court orders*. In short: state can promote, Parliament can legislate, courts can guide - but citizens cannot be directly ordered/punished for a duty unless a specific law is violated.

Trap: "Non-justiciable but not toothless." "Justiciable" = Rights. "Non-justiciable" = DPSP + Duties.

## 15. Duties vs Rights vs DPSP - One Table

| Point | Fundamental Rights (Part III) | DPSP (Part IV) | Fundamental Duties (Part IVA) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whose job? | Citizen claims from State | State must follow while ruling | Citizen must follow towards nation |
| Enforceable? | Yes, by writs (Art 32/226) | No, non-justiciable | No, non-justiciable |
| Nature | Political/civil freedoms | Social/economic goals for govt | Moral/civic obligations |
| Example | Art 19 speech, Art 21 life | Art 48A environment by State | Art 51A(g) environment by citizen |
| Exam link | Justiciable | 42nd + environment = pair with duties | USSR, Swaran Singh, 42nd, 86th |

Memory trick: **Rights = State to Citizen. DPSP = Citizen expects State to act. Duties = Citizen to Nation.**

## 16. SI-Duty Applications: How to Use This Chapter on Ground

1. Flag/anthem functions (a): Brief constables on尊 respect protocol, crowd control without chaos.
2. Unity/harmony (c+e): Preventive patrol in mixed localities, social media watch for hate speech.
3. Women dignity (e): Zero-delay FIR, victim sensitivity, coordination with Sakhi centres.
4. Environment (g): Night check on sand lorries, red sanders info network, festival pollution compliance.
5. Science (h): Crackdown on superstition gangs, rumour-busting via village meetings.
6. Public property (i): Video evidence during dharnas, PDPP Act booking, protecting RTC/police vehicles.
7. Education (k): Rescue child labour, enrolment drives with education dept.

In Mains/descriptive: quote "Rights and duties are two sides of same coin" + cite Verma Committee + 86th Amendment balance (21A Right + 51A-k Duty).

## 17. Final 60-Second Revision

- 1976 + Swaran Singh + 42nd + USSR + Part IVA + 51A + 10 duties. 2002 + 86th + 1 duty (k) = 11.
- a-flag, b-freedom ideals, c-sovereignty, d-defend, e-harmony/women, f-culture, g-environment, h-science, i-property/non-violence, j-excellence, k-6-14 schooling.
- Non-justiciable, Verma 1999, 2024 - nurtured not commanded.

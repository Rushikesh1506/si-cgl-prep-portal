# 🔍 AUDIT REPORT — AP Police SI GS Pack

**Files audited:** day01.md · polity_days01_06.json · polity_days07_12.json · day01_quiz.json · gs_bank.json · syllabus_plan.json

**Verdict:** Structure is sound. Content is **B-grade** — correct on the easy 70%, wrong or dated on the 30% that decides rank.

## ⚠️ SECTION 1 — FACTUAL CORRECTIONS (must-fix)

### ⚠️ C1 — Art 15(2): the "State funds" error

**Original** (day01.md §3 and day01_quiz Q11 explanation): "15(2) covers shops/public resorts **maintained by state funds**."

**Correct:** Art 15(2) has **two limbs, and only the second carries the funding condition**:

- **15(2)(a)** — access to **shops, public restaurants, hotels, places of public entertainment**. **No funding condition.** Applies to **privately owned** shops and hotels. This is one of the rare Fundamental Rights that operates **horizontally** (citizen against citizen).
- **15(2)(b)** — use of **wells, tanks, bathing ghats, roads, places of public resort** — *these* must be **wholly or partly maintained out of State funds**, or dedicated to the public.

**Why it matters:** the original rule would make a private hotel refusing a caste group lawful. It is not — that is a direct 15(2)(a) violation, and for an SI it is also a cognisable offence under the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act. The final answer in Q11 (a private *home* is not covered) stays correct, but the reasoning taught is wrong.

### ⚠️ C2 — Lok Sabha maximum strength

**Original** (Day 7): "LS max 552 (530 states + 20 UT + 2 Anglo-Indian REMOVED by 104th)" — self-contradictory.

**Correct:** 552 was the **pre-2020 ceiling**. The 104th Amendment ended Anglo-Indian nomination with effect from **25 January 2020**. The constitutional maximum today is **550** (530 States + 20 UTs). Present actual strength is **543**.

### ⚠️ C3 — Preventive detention: the 2-month rule that never came into force

**Original** (Day 2): "Board review" — unqualified.

**Correct:** The **44th Amendment's** change (Advisory Board reference within **2 months**, chaired by a **sitting** High Court judge) was **passed but never brought into force** — no commencement notification was ever issued. The operative rule remains the **original 3 months** under Art 22(4), with a Board chaired by a person **qualified to be** a High Court judge.

**Trap value:** the highest in the entire preventive-detention topic. Any option saying "2 months" is **wrong**.

### ⚠️ C4 — The 50% ceiling is no longer absolute

**Original** (Day 1 and day01_quiz Q14): "50% ceiling (Indra Sawhney), breach only in extraordinary cases."

**Correct today:** **Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India (2022)** upheld the 103rd Amendment 3:2 and held that (i) the 50% ceiling is **not part of the basic structure**, and (ii) the **10% EWS quota sits outside the 50% limit**. So "reservation can never exceed 50%" is now a **false** statement. Indra Sawhney's ceiling binds **Art 16(4) backward-class reservation**, not EWS.

### ⚠️ C5 — Art 31C is not where Minerva Mills left it

**Original** (Days 1 and 3): stops at Minerva Mills, 1980.

**Correct:** **Property Owners Association v. State of Maharashtra (November 2024, 9 judges)** held that the **unamended Art 31C survives**, and narrowed **Art 39(b)** — **not every privately owned resource is a "material resource of the community"**, overruling the expansive Ranganatha Reddy / Sanjeev Coke line.

### ⚠️ C6 — Art 12 and the judiciary

**Original** (day01.md §1): "State = government + parliament + **courts** + authorities."

**Correct:** the judiciary is "State" in its **administrative and rule-making** capacity only. Its **judicial orders are not challengeable as Fundamental Rights violations** (*Naresh Shridhar Mirajkar*, 1967; *A.R. Antulay*, 1988). Writing "courts" flatly manufactures a wrong answer.

### ⚠️ C7 — Governor's assent: the position changed in November 2025

**Original** (Day 6): "assent 200 (options + reservation)" — silent on the 2023–25 litigation.

**Sequence to know:**

1. *State of Punjab v. Principal Secretary to Governor* (2023) — a Governor cannot sit on Bills indefinitely.
2. *State of Tamil Nadu v. Governor of Tamil Nadu* (**April 2025**) — the Court fixed **timelines** and read in **"deemed assent"**.
3. **Presidential Reference under Art 143, opinion delivered 20 November 2025 (5-judge Bench)** — held that courts **cannot judicially prescribe timelines** for the Governor or President under Arts 200/201, and that **"deemed assent" is impermissible**. Prolonged unexplained inaction remains justiciable, but the remedy is a **direction to decide**, not deemed assent.

**Exam line:** any material still teaching "deemed assent" is **out of date**. ⚠️ This is the fastest-moving item in the pack — re-verify in the month before the exam.

### ⚠️ C8 — The AP Reorganisation Act, 2014 did NOT name Amaravati

**Original** (gs_bank #3): "2014 Act; Amaravati as capital."

**Correct:** The Act provided **Hyderabad as common capital for not more than 10 years** (that period **ended 2 June 2024**) and directed the Centre to constitute an **expert committee** — the **Sivaramakrishnan Committee** (report August 2014), which did **not** recommend a single mega-capital. **Amaravati was chosen by the AP Government in 2015**, not by the 2014 Act.

### ⚠️ C9 — India's 2011 literacy rate

**Original** (gs_bank #25): "national ~74%."

**Correct:** **India 72.98% (≈73%)**; undivided AP **67.02%** — the lowest among the major States. Residuary AP is approximately **67.4%**.

### ⚠️ C10 — Polavaram's district is wrong after 2022

**Original** (gs_bank #7): "Enters at East Godavari region; Polavaram project."

**Correct after the 4 April 2022 reorganisation:** the **Polavaram project site is in Eluru district** (it was in West Godavari). The Godavari **enters AP through Alluri Sitharama Raju district** — via the **seven mandals transferred from Khammam** to AP under the AP Reorganisation Act, 2014. "East Godavari" is a pre-2022 answer.

### ⚠️ C11 — Fundamental Duties count

**Original** (gs_bank #16): "Article 51A, added by 42nd Amendment. 51A = 11 duties." This reads as though the 42nd gave 11.

**Correct:** the **42nd (1976) gave 10**. The **11th — 51A(k), the parent's duty to provide education to a child aged 6–14 — was added by the 86th (2002)**. Total **11**.

### ⚠️ C12 — Andhra Mahasabha: two different bodies

**Original** (gs_bank #9): "First Andhra Mahasabha 1913 Bapatla."

**Correct and complete:** that is the **Andhra Mahasabha of coastal Andhra (Madras Presidency), Bapatla, May 1913, presided over by B.N. Sarma**. A **different** body — the **Andhra Maha Sabha of Hyderabad State (Telangana)** — first met at **Jogipet, Medak, in 1930**. Examiners deliberately swap these two.

### ⚠️ C13 — Sabarimala is not settled law

**Original** (Day 11): cited as though decided.

**Correct:** the 2018 judgment (4:1) went into **review**; in **November 2019** the questions were referred to a **9-judge bench** and **remain pending**. Teach it as **unresolved**.

### ⚠️ C14 — The Day 6 VP-vacancy question is malformed

The option *"President acts via RS Dy Chair"* is meaningless.

**Correct:** there is **no "acting Vice-President"**. When the **Vice-President's** office falls vacant, the **Deputy Chairman** (or a Rajya Sabha member authorised by the President) discharges the **Chairman's** duties, and an election is held as soon as possible. The **Vice-President acts as PRESIDENT** when the **President's** office is vacant (Art 65) — for a **maximum of 6 months**.

## 🕳️ SECTION 2 — MAJOR GAPS (topics absent that will be asked)

### 🔴 GAP 1 — Article 371D (the largest omission in the pack)

Twelve days of AP Polity with **no mention of Art 371D** — the provision that governs **how AP public employment, including police recruitment, is legally structured**.

- Inserted by the **32nd Amendment, 1973**, following the **Six-Point Formula (1973)** that settled the **Jai Andhra / Mulki agitation**.
- Empowers the **President** — not Parliament, not the State — to provide for **local cadres** and **reservation in direct recruitment by local area**.
- Executed through the **AP Public Employment (Organisation of Local Cadres and Regulation of Direct Recruitment) Order, 1975** — "the Presidential Order".
- Provides for an **Administrative Tribunal** for AP service disputes.
- Survives bifurcation: **applies to both AP and Telangana**.

**Why it is a certainty:** it is the only Article in the Constitution written *for Andhra Pradesh*, and it is the legal basis of the candidate's own recruitment. It is also the perfect **Art 16(3) contrast** — 16(3) puts a residence condition in **Parliament's** hands; 371D puts local-area reservation in a **Presidential Order**.

### 🔴 GAP 2 — PESA Act, 1996

Day 9 teaches the 73rd and 243M but never says that **Part IX does not apply to Fifth Schedule Scheduled Areas**, which is precisely why **PESA 1996** was enacted. AP **has** Scheduled Areas (tracts in **Alluri Sitharama Raju, Parvathipuram Manyam, Eluru, Palnadu, Srikakulam**). Gram Sabha consent, control over land alienation and minor forest produce are **live field issues for an SI posted there**.

### 🔴 GAP 3 — The amendment spine stops too early

Day 10 ends at the 104th. Missing:

- **102nd (2018)** — constitutional status to the NCBC, **Art 338B**, **Art 342A**.
- **105th (2021)** — restored **the States'** power to identify SEBCs.
- **106th (2023) — Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam** — **33% reservation for women** in the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies via **Arts 330A / 332A / 334A**; **operative only after the next Census and the delimitation that follows it**.

The 106th is the **single most probable amendment question** in a 2026 cycle.

### 🔴 GAP 4 — Art 169 has an AP story the pack skips

Day 7 says "AP has a Council (58 members)". It omits the part that carries the exam: the AP Legislative Council was **abolished in 1985**, **revived in 2007**, and on **27 January 2020 the AP Assembly passed a resolution to abolish it again — Parliament never acted on it, so the Council continues.** That *is* the Art 169 question: **an Assembly resolution alone achieves nothing; only Parliament's law abolishes.**

### 🔴 GAP 5 — The new criminal codes get one line each

gs_bank #13–15 give only the names. For a **police** exam this is the highest-yield law block, and it needs: **in force 1 July 2024**; **BNS 358 sections, BNSS 531, BSA 170**; **Zero FIR (BNSS s.173)**; **e-FIR**; **mandatory forensic visit for offences punishable with 7 years or more (s.176(3))**; **90-day progress update to the informant**; **s.187 police custody restructured**; **BNS s.111 organised crime**; **s.113 terrorist act**; **s.103(2) mob lynching**; **s.106(2) hit-and-run**.

### 🟠 Smaller gaps

- **Art 358 vs 359** is never contrasted sharply (Day 5).
- **Art 329 and Art 243-O** — the bar on judicial interference in elections. Direct relevance to SI election duty.
- **NITI Aayog is an executive body; the Finance Commission is constitutional (Art 280)** — the standard pairing trap. The **16th Finance Commission** (Arvind Panagariya, award period **2026–31**) is live.
- **Art 246A** — the actual GST taxing power. gs_bank #26 gives only 279A.
- **Deltaic alluvial soil** is missing from gs_bank #22 — it is AP's rice bowl and the most asked soil in the State.

## 🧨 SECTION 3 — STRUCTURAL WEAKNESSES

1. **Format monotony.** Of roughly 120 quiz items, about **70 use the same "S1/S2 → S1 only / S2 only / Both / Neither"** shell. The candidate learns the *pattern*, not the *concept* — and APSLPRB does not use one template.
2. **Distractors are dead weight.** Options such as "CJI", "CAG", "Governor" on a question about Art 35 are not distractors; they are filler. A real distractor has to be **almost right**.
3. **Explanations state the answer, not the trap.** "Rural-29, urban-18" teaches nothing to a candidate who did not already know it.
4. **No difficulty labelling**, so there is no way to run a weak-area drill.
5. **Zero field application.** This is a **police** exam, and not one question places the candidate at a scene.
6. **AP content is decorative** — one AP line appended per lesson. AP should be *driving* the question.
7. **day01.md is malformed** — it jumps from §5 to §7 (there is no §6), and §4 contains corrupted text: *"Ministers' official acts,AA lodges? No - only listed ones."*
8. **Bot truncation risk.** `bot.py` hard-truncates the poll question to **290** characters, each option to **100**, and the explanation to **190**. Rich explanations are **silently chopped mid-sentence**. Upgraded JSON must be written to those limits — full explanations belong in the Markdown modules, not in the poll.

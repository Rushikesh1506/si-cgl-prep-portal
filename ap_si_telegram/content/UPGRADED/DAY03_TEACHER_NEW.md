# Tomorrow: DPSP & Fundamental Duties — Full Teacher Lesson

## What is this topic, in one breath
Yesterday you learned rights you can demand in court. Today you learn the opposite: goals the Constitution gives the GOVERNMENT, which no citizen can directly demand in court — plus duties it expects from YOU, which no court can directly punish you for skipping. Rights are your sword. DPSP is the government's compass. Duties are your conscience. Exams love this topic because students mix up which is which.

## Subtopics inside
1. What DPSP means and why it cannot be taken to court (Article 37).
2. Three baskets: Socialist, Gandhian, Liberal principles.
3. The narrow shield: which DPSP laws survive rights-challenge (Art 31C, Minerva Mills).
4. Fundamental Duties: ten plus one, where they came from, how they are enforced.
5. How rights, DPSP and duties collide — and who wins.

## Why this topic exists — the story
When India became free, leaders faced a painful truth. The treasury was empty, villages were poor, courts were few. If the Constitution promised every poor person a job, house and hospital as a court-enforceable right, courts would drown on day one and the government would go bankrupt. So they split the dream in two. Part III gave a few urgent freedoms as enforceable rights. Part IV listed the rest as Directive Principles — directions to future governments: build welfare step by step as money allows. Think of rights as today's cooked food and DPSP as seeds for tomorrow's crop. A hungry man cannot eat seeds today, but without seeds there is no food tomorrow.

## Basket 1 — Socialist: reduce the gap between rich and poor
The state shall secure social order with justice, minimise income gaps, and ensure: livelihood for all, shared ownership of resources, no wealth piling in few hands, equal pay for equal work, protection of workers' health, care for children, free legal aid for the poor, work and education help, maternity relief, living wage, worker participation in management, and cooperatives. The two most exam-beating entries: free legal aid (Art 39A) and the resource-sharing pair Art 39(b)-(c) — because only THOSE TWO give laws a shield against rights-challenge. A law about anything else welfare-flavoured gets no shield. Remember: shield is narrow, only two clauses.

## Basket 2 — Gandhian: villages and the weak
Organise village panchayats. Promote cottage industries. Protect SC, ST and weaker sections in education and jobs. Improve nutrition and public health. Control intoxicating drinks except for medicine. Protect cows, farming and animal care. When your brother raids illicit liquor near a school or rescues child labour, he is walking this basket on duty.

## Basket 3 — Liberal: modern ideas
Common civil code for all citizens. Early childhood care below age six (note: ages six to fourteen moved to the RIGHT to education in 2002 — examiner traps this split every time). Protect environment, forests and wildlife. Protect monuments. Separate judiciary from executive. Promote world peace. The environment and judiciary-separation entries matter for police: green laws you enforce, and the magistrate who must independently check your remand diary.

## The shield, told simply
Normally if a law breaks a fundamental right, courts strike it. But laws genuinely sharing resources and stopping wealth concentration get a shield — courts won't strike them for breaking equality or speech freedoms. In 1976 Parliament stretched this shield to cover ALL welfare laws. In 1980 the Minerva Mills case cut it back to only the two resource clauses. In 2024 nine judges narrowed the meaning of community resources further — not all private property counts. So the rule today: shield only for true 39(b)-(c) laws. A liquor-ban law or housing scheme claiming the shield fails — wrong basket.

## Fundamental Duties — ten plus one
In 1976 ten duties were added: respect Constitution, flag and anthem; follow freedom ideals; protect unity; defend country; promote harmony beyond religion and language; value culture; protect environment and animals; develop scientific thinking; safeguard public property, avoid violence; strive for excellence; and in 2002 one more — every parent must send children aged six to fourteen to school. No court will jail you directly for skipping them. But Parliament can make laws enforcing them — flag code, environment fines, schooling law. When youth burn buses, your brother counsels them in this language first, books them second.

## Collision: rights vs DPSP vs duties
A housing law takes land; owner cries equality, state cries welfare. Who wins? Courts balance. Rights generally prevail, but harmony is attempted — Minerva Mills said this balance itself cannot be destroyed. Duties never defeat rights; they guide laws. In one line: rights are enforceable, DPSP guiding, duties inspiring — and the shield is the only bridge where DPSP beats rights, that too only two clauses wide.

## How examiner twists this (read twice)
Trap 1: "DPSP enforceable by writ" — false, Art 37 bars it. Trap 2: "Art 45 covers ages six to fourteen" — false after 2002, it covers below six only. Trap 3: "All welfare laws get the shield" — false, only two clauses. Trap 4: "Duties punishable directly" — false, needs a law. Trap 5: swapping 42nd and 44th, swapping 91st (ministers cap) with 103rd (EWS). Trap 6: calling the 2024 ruling a takeover of all private property — false, it narrowed the tag.

## Memory anchors
Three baskets: SOCIAL (38 to 43B), GANDHI (40, 43, 46, 47, 48), LIBERAL (44, 45, 48A, 49, 50, 51). Shield = two clauses only. Duties = ten plus schooling. Ages: below six directive, six-to-fourteen right-plus-duty. Night line: rights you demand, DPSP government follows, duties you follow.

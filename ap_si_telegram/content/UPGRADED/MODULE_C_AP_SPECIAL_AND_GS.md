# 📚 TOPIC 1 — ARTICLE 371D: THE ARTICLE WRITTEN FOR ANDHRA PRADESH

## 1️⃣ Core Concept

**Foundation.** Andhra Pradesh is one of a small group of States with a **special constitutional provision written specifically for it**. That provision is **Article 371D**, and it governs **how public employment and education are allocated within the State** — including the recruitment the candidate is sitting for.

**Why it exists.** When Andhra State (1953) merged with the Telangana region of Hyderabad State to form Andhra Pradesh on **1 November 1956**, the **Gentlemen's Agreement (1956)** promised safeguards for Telangana in employment and education. Those safeguards, rooted in the older **Mulki Rules**, were repeatedly contested. The dispute erupted as the **Telangana agitation (1969)** and then the **Jai Andhra agitation (1972–73)**; Andhra Pradesh was placed under **President's Rule in January 1973**.

The settlement was the **Six-Point Formula of September 1973**, and to give it constitutional cover Parliament enacted the **Constitution (Thirty-second Amendment) Act, 1973**, inserting **Arts 371D and 371E**.

## 2️⃣ 🔑 Technical Terms

| Term | Simple meaning | Exam relevance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mulki Rules | Old Hyderabad State rules reserving jobs for locals | The historical root of 371D |
| Six-Point Formula | The 1973 political settlement between the regions | The immediate cause of the 32nd Amendment |
| Local cadre | A pool of posts organised by local area rather than statewide | The mechanism 371D authorises |
| Presidential Order 1975 | The instrument executing Art 371D | The operative document, not the Article alone |
| Art 371E | Establishment of a **Central University** in AP | Became the **University of Hyderabad** |

## 3️⃣ 🧠 Advanced Understanding

**What Art 371D actually does:**

- It empowers the **President**, by order, to provide for **equitable opportunities and facilities** for people of different parts of the State in matters of **public employment and education**.
- The President may require the State Government to **organise civil posts into local cadres** for different parts of the State, and may specify **local areas** and provide for **direct recruitment to posts in a local cadre**.
- It authorises the constitution of an **Administrative Tribunal** for the State to deal with service disputes, with the Tribunal's orders subject to the State Government's power of modification or annulment in specified circumstances.
- Art 371D(10) gives the Article and any order under it **overriding effect** over other provisions of the Constitution and other laws.

**The instrument that matters: the AP Public Employment (Organisation of Local Cadres and Regulation of Direct Recruitment) Order, 1975** — universally called "the Presidential Order". It defines what makes a candidate a **local candidate** of a zone, and how posts are allocated between local and non-local candidates.

**Three high-value exam contrasts:**

| Provision | Who may prescribe | What is prescribed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Art 16(3)** | **Parliament**, by law | A **residence** requirement for a class of posts in a State |
| **Art 371D** | The **President**, by order | **Local cadres** and **local-area reservation** in direct recruitment |
| **Art 16(4)** | The **State**, by executive order or law | Reservation for **backward classes** |

That table is the question. Candidates who know only Art 16(3) will answer "Parliament" to a 371D stem and lose the mark.

**After bifurcation.** The **AP Reorganisation Act, 2014** kept Art 371D alive: it **continues to apply to both Andhra Pradesh and Telangana**. The Presidential Order was amended to work with the two successor States. Any option saying that 371D lapsed on bifurcation is false.

**Art 371E** — the companion provision — required Parliament to provide for the establishment of a **Central University in Andhra Pradesh**. That obligation produced the **University of Hyderabad (1974)**. Art 371E is the only "special provision" article whose content is a **single university**, which is why it is asked as a curiosity.

## 4️⃣ 👮 Field / Real-World Application

A candidate recruited as an SI is allotted to a particular zone and questions why the merit list was drawn zone-wise rather than statewide.

- The answer is **not** Art 16(3), which concerns a **residence requirement prescribed by Parliament**.
- It is **Art 371D**, executed through the **Presidential Order, 1975**, under which posts are organised into **local cadres** and a proportion of direct recruitment vacancies is filled by **local candidates** of the zone.
- Service grievances arising from that allocation fall within the **Administrative Tribunal** machinery contemplated by 371D.

**The transferable lesson:** an officer who cannot name the constitutional basis of his own appointment cannot explain the legality of a posting, a transfer or a seniority dispute. **Art 371D is professional knowledge, not trivia.**

## 5️⃣ 🔗 Concept Connections

Art 3 and the reorganisation of States → the **SRC** and the **7th Amendment** → the **Gentlemen's Agreement 1956** → the 1969 and 1972–73 agitations → **President's Rule in AP, January 1973** (Day 5) → the **Six-Point Formula** → the **32nd Amendment** → **Arts 371D and 371E** → **Art 16(3)** contrast (Day 1) → the **AP Reorganisation Act, 2014** → Part XIV and Part XIVA tribunals.

## 6️⃣ 📝 ADVANCED QUESTIONS

### Q1 — Multi-statement
**Difficulty:** 🟠 Level 3 — Advanced SI

Consider the following statements regarding Article 371D:

1. It was inserted by the 32nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1973.
2. It empowers Parliament to prescribe a residence requirement for public employment in Andhra Pradesh.
3. It provides for the constitution of an Administrative Tribunal for the State.
4. It ceased to apply to Andhra Pradesh after the reorganisation of 2014.

Which are correct?

A) 1 and 3 only
B) 1, 2 and 3 only
C) 1, 3 and 4 only
D) 2 and 3 only

**✅ Answer: A**

**🧠 Explanation.** 1 and 3 are correct — the 32nd Amendment of 1973 inserted Art 371D, and the Article expressly contemplates an **Administrative Tribunal** for the State.

**❌ Distractor Analysis**
- **Statement 2** — the key trap. **Art 16(3)** is the provision under which **Parliament** may prescribe a **residence** requirement. Art 371D operates through a **Presidential Order**, and its subject is **local cadres and local-area recruitment**, not residence as such. The two are routinely conflated.
- **Statement 4** — false. The **AP Reorganisation Act, 2014** preserved Art 371D for **both** successor States.
- **Option B** is for the candidate who knows 371D exists but has merged it with Art 16(3).

**🎯 Examiner's Trap.** Two provisions that both restrict recruitment by geography, with **different authorities and different instruments**. The discriminator is **who acts — Parliament by law, or the President by order**.

**⚡ 30-Second Shortcut.** **"16(3) is Parliament's law; 371D is the President's order."** Read the option for the **actor** before reading it for the subject.

### Q2 — Chronological order
**Difficulty:** 🟠 Level 3 — Advanced SI

Arrange the following in chronological order:

1. Formation of Andhra Pradesh
2. The Gentlemen's Agreement
3. The Six-Point Formula
4. Insertion of Article 371D in the Constitution

A) 2 – 1 – 3 – 4
B) 1 – 2 – 3 – 4
C) 2 – 1 – 4 – 3
D) 1 – 3 – 2 – 4

**✅ Answer: A**

**🧠 Explanation.** The **Gentlemen's Agreement** was concluded in **1956 before** the merger, as its precondition. **Andhra Pradesh was formed on 1 November 1956.** The **Six-Point Formula** came in **September 1973**, and the **32nd Amendment inserting Art 371D** followed in **1973**, to give the Formula constitutional backing.

**❌ Distractor Analysis**
- **B)** places the Agreement after the formation. The Agreement was the **condition on which** the merger proceeded — the commonest error in AP history.
- **C)** reverses the Formula and the Amendment, inverting cause and effect: the Amendment exists **because of** the Formula.
- **D)** places the 1973 Formula before the 1956 Agreement.

**🎯 Examiner's Trap.** Both a **cause–effect reversal** (option C) and a **precondition inversion** (option B) in one question. Whenever an agreement and a formation appear together, ask **which one made the other possible**.

**⚡ 30-Second Shortcut.** **"Agreement before merger; Formula before Amendment."** Political settlements precede the legal instruments that implement them, every time.

## 7️⃣ 📌 Final Takeaway

1. **Art 371D came from the 32nd Amendment, 1973**, out of the Six-Point Formula and the Jai Andhra agitation.
2. **16(3) = Parliament by law. 371D = the President by order.**
3. **The operative document is the Presidential Order, 1975** — local cadres, local candidates, an Administrative Tribunal.
4. **371D survived the 2014 bifurcation** and applies to both AP and Telangana. **371E gave AP a Central University** — the University of Hyderabad.

# 📚 TOPIC 2 — ANDHRA PRADESH: FORMATION AND POLITICAL HISTORY

## 1️⃣ Core Concept

**The sequence, with the dates that get asked:**

- **1913, May — the first Andhra Mahasabha at Bapatla**, presided over by **B.N. Sarma**, demanding a separate Andhra province out of the Madras Presidency.
- **1937 — the Sri Bagh Pact**, an agreement between the leaders of coastal Andhra and Rayalaseema on the distribution of the capital, the High Court and the university, to reassure Rayalaseema about the merger. The parallel of the later Gentlemen's Agreement.
- **1948 — the Dar Commission** advised **against** reorganisation on a purely linguistic basis.
- **1949 — the JVP Committee** (**J**awaharlal Nehru, **V**allabhbhai Patel, **P**attabhi Sitaramayya) broadly agreed with Dar, but conceded that an Andhra province might be formed if Madras city were given up.
- **19 October 1952 — Potti Sriramulu begins a fast unto death** for a separate Andhra State; **he dies on 15 December 1952.**
- **1 October 1953 — Andhra State is formed**, with **Kurnool as capital** and the **High Court at Guntur**. **It was India's first State created on a linguistic basis.**
- **1953 — the States Reorganisation Commission** is appointed under **Fazl Ali**, with **K.M. Panikkar** and **H.N. Kunzru**; it reports in **1955**.
- **1956 — the Gentlemen's Agreement** between Andhra and Telangana leaders.
- **1 November 1956 — Andhra Pradesh is formed** by merging Andhra State with the Telugu-speaking areas of Hyderabad State, under the **States Reorganisation Act** and the **7th Amendment**. Capital: **Hyderabad**. First Chief Minister: **Neelam Sanjiva Reddy**.
- **1969 and 1972–73 — the Telangana and Jai Andhra agitations**; **President's Rule in January 1973**; the **Six-Point Formula**; the **32nd Amendment** and **Art 371D**.
- **2 June 2014 — the AP Reorganisation Act, 2014** takes effect; Telangana becomes the 29th State.
- **2015 — Amaravati announced as the capital** by the AP Government.
- **4 April 2022 — AP reorganised from 13 into 26 districts.**

⚠️ **Correction (gs_bank #3).** The **AP Reorganisation Act, 2014 did not name Amaravati**. It provided **Hyderabad as the common capital for not more than ten years** — a period that **ended on 2 June 2024** — and required the Centre to constitute an expert committee. The **Sivaramakrishnan Committee** (report August 2014) did **not** recommend a single mega-capital. Amaravati was a **decision of the AP Government in 2015**.

⚠️ **Correction (gs_bank #9).** Distinguish two bodies with confusingly similar names: the **Andhra Mahasabha** of coastal Andhra (**Bapatla, 1913**) and the **Andhra Maha Sabha of Hyderabad State**, first held at **Jogipet, Medak, in 1930**. Examiners swap them deliberately.

# 📚 TOPIC 3 — ANDHRA PRADESH GEOGRAPHY

## 1️⃣ Core Concept

**Location and extent.** Andhra Pradesh lies on the **east coast**, with the **second-longest coastline among Indian States — about 974 km — after Gujarat**. It borders **Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Telangana, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu**, and has a short boundary with **Puducherry (Yanam)**. **26 districts** since **4 April 2022**.

**Rivers.**
- **Godavari** — the longest river in AP and the **longest in peninsular India**, rising at **Trimbakeshwar, Nashik**. It enters AP through **Alluri Sitharama Raju district**, via the **seven mandals transferred from Khammam** under the AP Reorganisation Act, 2014. It divides into the **Gautami** and **Vasishta** branches near **Dowleswaram** to form its delta. Major structures: the **Polavaram Project** and the **Sir Arthur Cotton Barrage at Dowleswaram**.
- **Krishna** — rises at **Mahabaleshwar**. Key projects in AP: **Srisailam**, **Nagarjuna Sagar** and the **Prakasam Barrage at Vijayawada**. Chief tributaries: the **Tungabhadra**, **Bhima** and **Musi**.
- **Penna (Pennar)** — rises in the **Nandi Hills, Karnataka**; the **Somasila** and **Gandikota** projects lie on it. It is the principal river of **Rayalaseema**.
- Others: **Vamsadhara**, **Nagavali**, **Sarada**.

⚠️ **Correction (gs_bank #7).** After the 2022 reorganisation the **Polavaram Project site is in ELURU district** — it was in West Godavari. "East Godavari" is a pre-2022 answer.

**Soils.** ⚠️ **The original card gives only red and black.** The complete position:
- **Red soils** — the most extensive, covering roughly two-thirds of the State, dominant in **Rayalaseema** and the uplands. Suited to groundnut, millets and pulses.
- **Black (regur) soils** — parts of **Kurnool, Nandyal, Anantapur, Prakasam and Guntur**. Moisture-retentive; **cotton**.
- **Deltaic alluvial soils** — the **Godavari and Krishna deltas**. The most fertile, and the basis of AP's **rice bowl**. **Omitting this is the most consequential gap in the original geography card.**
- **Coastal sands** and **laterite** in patches.

**Other essentials.** **Arma Konda** (also written Jindhagada) in the **Eastern Ghats, Alluri Sitharama Raju district**, is the **highest peak in the Eastern Ghats and in AP**. **Kolleru Lake**, between the Godavari and Krishna deltas, is a **Ramsar site**; **Pulicat Lake**, shared with Tamil Nadu, is India's **second-largest brackish-water lagoon** and adjoins **Sriharikota**. **Ports: Visakhapatnam** (the major port, and the **only natural harbour on the east coast**), with **Krishnapatnam, Gangavaram, Kakinada and Machilipatnam** among the others. **Satish Dhawan Space Centre, SHAR, Sriharikota**, lies in **Tirupati district** (previously Nellore district).

# 📚 TOPIC 4 — THE NEW CRIMINAL LAWS (highest-yield block for a police exam)

## 1️⃣ Core Concept

⚠️ **The original pack gives one line each.** For an SI paper this is the block that most rewards detail.

The three colonial-era codes were replaced with effect from **1 July 2024**:

| Replaced | Replacement | Sections |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Indian Penal Code, 1860 | **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS)** | **358** |
| Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 | **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS)** | **531** |
| Indian Evidence Act, 1872 | **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (BSA)** | **170** |

**Note the order of the pairings.** BNS replaces the **substantive** law (what is an offence); BNSS replaces the **procedural** law (how it is investigated and tried); BSA replaces the **evidentiary** law (what may be proved and how). Examiners swap BNS and BNSS in the options constantly.

## 2️⃣ 🧠 Advanced Understanding — what actually changed

**In the BNSS (procedure — the SI's daily law):**
- **Zero FIR** is given statutory form: an FIR may be registered **irrespective of the territorial jurisdiction** of the police station and then transferred.
- **e-FIR / electronic registration** of information is recognised, with the informant required to sign it within a prescribed period.
- **Forensic investigation is mandatory** for offences punishable with **seven years' imprisonment or more** — a forensic team **must** visit the scene and the process is to be **videographed**.
- The informant must be given a **progress update within 90 days**.
- **Police custody** is restructured: the fifteen days of police custody may be sought **in parts within the initial 40 or 60 days** of the overall remand period, rather than only in the first fifteen days.
- **Trial in absentia** is provided for proclaimed offenders.
- **Timelines** are prescribed for framing charges and for pronouncing judgment.
- **Summons may be served electronically.**
- **Mercy petitions** in death-sentence cases are given a statutory procedure.

**In the BNS (new and restructured offences):**
- **Section 111 — organised crime**, defined for the first time in general criminal law.
- **Section 112 — petty organised crime.**
- **Section 113 — terrorist act**, brought into the general penal code.
- **Section 103(2) — murder by a group of five or more on grounds of race, caste, community, sex, place of birth, language or personal belief** — the provision addressing **mob lynching**.
- **Section 106(2) — causing death by rash and negligent driving and fleeing without reporting** — the hit-and-run provision.
- **Sedition is removed**; **Section 152** creates the offence of **endangering the sovereignty, unity and integrity of India**.
- **Community service** is introduced as a form of punishment.
- **Snatching** is made a distinct offence.

**In the BSA (evidence):**
- **Electronic and digital records** are placed on the same footing as documentary evidence, with an expanded definition covering information in semiconductor memory, and on communication and computing devices.
- The certificate requirement for electronic records is retained and restructured.

### Q2 — Case / Situation based
**Difficulty:** 🟠 Level 3 — Advanced SI

Information is received at a police station about an offence punishable with ten years' imprisonment, committed within the limits of a different police station in another district.

Which of the following is the correct course under the BNSS?

A) Refuse registration and direct the informant to the police station having jurisdiction
B) Register the FIR irrespective of jurisdiction and transfer it to the station concerned, and ensure a forensic team visits the scene
C) Register the FIR only after obtaining the permission of the Superintendent of Police
D) Record the information in the station diary, jurisdiction being a condition precedent to registration

**✅ Answer: B**

**🧠 Explanation.** **Zero FIR** is now statutory under the **BNSS** — an FIR may be registered **regardless of territorial jurisdiction** and then transferred to the competent station. Separately, because the offence is punishable with **seven years or more**, **a forensic team's visit to the scene is mandatory**, and the process is to be videographed.

**❌ Distractor Analysis**
- **A)** the pre-BNSS practice that *Lalita Kumari* had already condemned, and which the BNSS now expressly forecloses. A **period transplant** distractor.
- **C)** invents a sanction requirement. No SP's permission is needed to register a cognisable offence.
- **D)** inverts the law: jurisdiction is **not** a condition precedent to registration.

**🎯 Examiner's Trap.** The question tests **two** BNSS changes at once — Zero FIR **and** the seven-year forensic threshold. Options A and C are each plausible to an officer trained on old practice.

**⚡ 30-Second Shortcut.** **"Seven years or more → forensic team compulsory."** And **"Zero FIR means jurisdiction never blocks registration."** Those two lines answer most BNSS procedural questions.

# 📚 TOPIC 5 — ECONOMY AND INSTITUTIONS

## 1️⃣ Core Concept — the deficits, decoded

| Term | Simple meaning | The precise formula |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Revenue Deficit** | Day-to-day spending exceeds day-to-day income | Revenue Expenditure − Revenue Receipts |
| **Fiscal Deficit** | Total spending exceeds total income other than borrowing | Total Expenditure − (Revenue Receipts + Non-debt Capital Receipts) |
| **Primary Deficit** | The fiscal deficit stripped of past interest | Fiscal Deficit − Interest Payments |
| **Effective Revenue Deficit** | Revenue deficit minus grants for capital asset creation | Revenue Deficit − Grants for creation of capital assets |

**Why it matters.** A **fiscal deficit is financed by borrowing**, so it **adds to public debt** — but the fiscal deficit is a **flow** in one year, while **public debt is a stock** accumulated over years. That distinction is the most-asked economics trap in State exams. A **primary deficit of zero** means the government is borrowing **only to pay interest on past borrowing**.

## 3️⃣ 📝 ADVANCED QUESTION

### Q1 — Assertion & Reason
**Difficulty:** 🟠 Level 3 — Advanced SI

**Assertion (A):** NITI Aayog's recommendations on the devolution of central taxes to the States are not binding on the Union Government.

**Reason (R):** NITI Aayog was constituted by a resolution of the Union Cabinet and is not a constitutional body.

A) Both A and R are true, and R correctly explains A
B) Both A and R are true, but R does not correctly explain A
C) A is false, but R is true
D) Both A and R are false

**✅ Answer: C**

**🧠 Explanation.** **R is true** — NITI Aayog rests on a **Cabinet resolution of 2015**, with no constitutional or statutory basis. **A is false**, but for a reason more fundamental than bindingness: **NITI Aayog has no role at all in the devolution of central taxes.** That function belongs exclusively to the **Finance Commission under Art 280**. The Assertion describes a power the body does not possess.

**❌ Distractor Analysis**
- **A)** the answer almost everyone chooses. Both statements sound right, and the causal link sounds natural — a non-constitutional body issuing non-binding advice. The flaw is **upstream**: the premise of the Assertion is false.
- **B)** also accepts the Assertion.
- **D)** wrongly rejects R, which is accurate.

**🎯 Examiner's Trap.** A **function transplanted to the wrong institution**, wrapped in an Assertion that is otherwise plausible. Candidates evaluate the *bindingness* claim and never question whether NITI Aayog has the function at all. **Always check the premise before checking the conclusion.**

**⚡ 30-Second Shortcut.** **Tax devolution belongs to the Finance Commission, always.** If any other body appears next to the word "devolution", the statement is false before you read the rest of it.

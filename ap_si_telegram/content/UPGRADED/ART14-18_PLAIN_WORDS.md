# Articles 14-18 in Plain Words

## Why equality comes first
Imagine a village with one well. The big landlord says only his family can drink from it. Others must walk miles. Everyone feels this is wrong, but nobody can question him. Our freedom fighters saw this everywhere — separate train compartments, separate schools, jobs only for some. So when they wrote our Constitution, the first promise they made was: every person is equal. That promise is Articles 14 to 18. Without it, all other rights belong only to rich and powerful people.

## Art 14 — same rule for everyone
Art 14 says the government cannot treat people unequally without a good reason. It has two simple meanings. First, nobody is above the law. If a minister and a bus driver commit the same mistake, both face the same court. Second, people in the same situation must get the same treatment. Two clerks doing the same work with the same salary must get the same pay.

But equal does not mean identical. A poor farmer and a rich businessman pay different tax because their situations are different. That is fair. What is unfair is picking one person for special bad treatment with no reason — like charging double tax only from houses painted red. Courts cancel such rules.

For any grouping to be fair, two questions must both get yes. First, is the difference real? Doctors and engineers are really different for a hospital allowance. Second, does that difference connect to the purpose? Hospital allowance for doctors connects to health work. Giving it only to engineers does not connect. If either answer is no, the rule breaks.

One more strong rule from judges. In 1974, an officer was denied promotion and no reason was written anywhere. The court said: government action with no reason is itself against equality. In 1978, a woman's passport was taken away without hearing her side. The court said: any procedure must be fair, with hearing and reasons. So whenever you see government acting without reason or without listening, that itself is a violation.

## Art 15 — only for citizens, five reasons banned
Art 15 protects citizens. It bans discrimination on exactly five grounds: religion, race, caste, sex, and place of birth. Say them slowly five times and they will never leave you.

It works in two places. First, shops, hotels and restaurants open to the public cannot refuse anyone on these grounds — even though these are private businesses. A hotel refusing a Dalit family food breaks this rule. Second, wells, ponds and roads maintained with government money cannot be closed to anyone. A private farm pond is different — it is fully private, so this rule does not reach it.

Art 15 also allows special help. Special schools and maternity care for women and children are allowed. Help for backward classes is allowed. Reservation in private colleges is allowed, except in minority-run colleges. Ten percent help for poor among forward classes is allowed. Some old books call this help an exception to equality. That is wrong, and examiners love this trap. In 1992, nine judges said this help IS equality in its true form — because treating unequal people exactly the same keeps them unequal forever.

Two words never come under Art 15: descent and residence. They belong only to the next Article about jobs. If a question mentions them, Art 15 can never be the answer.

## Art 16 — government jobs
Also citizens only. It repeats the same ban for jobs, but with seven grounds — the same five plus descent and residence. Only Parliament in Delhi can fix a rule that job seekers must live in a particular place. A state government order doing this is invalid. Reservation for backward classes is allowed here too, with total normally within fifty percent, and rich among backward classes kept out. Poor forward-class help sits in a separate ten percent. Your brother's own SI post comes through this Article combined with a special Andhra rule, so when he studies it, he is studying the law that created his own chance.

## Art 17 — one line, no exception
Untouchability is finished. Practising it in any form is a crime. No emergency, no custom, no excuse can save it. It applies against private people also, not only government. In 2020 the exam asked under which right it falls, and many marked wrongly. It sits under the Right to Equality, Articles 14 to 18.

## Art 18 — no royal titles
The government shall not give titles like Raja or Nawab, except army medals and university degrees. No citizen may accept a foreign title. National awards like Bharat Ratna are honours, not titles — the court said in 1996 you may receive them but must not stick them before your name.

## Three shields to remember
The President and Governors cannot be dragged to court while in office. During Emergency, most rights can be paused, but life and fair procedure can never be paused. Laws genuinely sharing village resources are protected from equality challenge, but only those two specific clauses, nothing wider.

## How to solve any question in one minute
First ask: is the person a foreigner? Then only Art 14 can help, because the next two Articles are citizens-only. Second: does the question mention descent or residence? Then go to the jobs Article. Third: words like immunity, emergency or welfare-shield? Jump to the three shields. Fourth: anyone says reservation is an exception to equality? Mark false with full confidence. Fifth: in match-the-following, fix untouchability first, then the equality-before-law phrase, and the rest falls in place by itself.

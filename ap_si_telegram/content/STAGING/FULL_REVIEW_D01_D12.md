## QUIZ (20)

**Q1** _(round F / trap I)_  A foreign national is refused a trade licence granted to comparable local applicants. He pleads both the freedom to carry on trade and the equality guarantees. Which guarantee is actually available to him on these facts?

- **[correct]** Equality before law and equal protection, available to every person
- [ ] Equality of opportunity in public employment, available to citizens
- [ ] Freedom to practise any trade or occupation, available to citizens
- [ ] Non-discrimination on the five listed grounds, available to citizens

*Explanation:* Only the person-based guarantee survives; trade freedom and the five-ground clause are citizens-only.

*Why:* The stem deliberately supplies a second, tempting route. The trade-freedom guarantee is confined to citizens exactly as the five-ground and employment clauses are, so it dies with them. The general guarantee binds all State action and speaks of 'person', which is why it alone survives.

*Trap note:* Read the rights-holder first. A foreigner in the stem extinguishes every citizens-only option at once, including trade freedom.

**Q2** _(round F / trap H)_  An eating house open to the travelling public turns a family away, stating caste as the reason. Which analysis of the constitutional position is correct?

- [ ] The listed-access guarantee applies only if the eating house draws State funding
- [ ] The general equality guarantee applies and binds the proprietor as a private party
- **[correct]** The listed-access guarantee applies and binds private parties with no funding condition
- [ ] The untouchability abolition alone reaches a purely private commercial refusal

*Explanation:* Eating houses sit in the sub-clause carrying no funding condition, and it binds private parties.

*Why:* The access guarantee has two limbs. Shops, restaurants, hotels and places of public entertainment carry NO funding condition and operate horizontally. Only the water-sources limb requires State maintenance or public dedication.

*Trap note:* The funding condition attaches to one limb only. Any option importing it into the shops-and-hotels limb is false.

**Q3** _(round F / trap C)_  A bathing tank maintained partly from panchayat funds and used by the general public is closed to Dalit households. Which analysis is correct?

- [ ] The water-sources limb fails: it requires maintenance wholly, not partly, from State funds
- [ ] The shops-and-hotels limb applies, no funding condition attaching to that limb
- [ ] Neither access limb applies; the general equality guarantee governs State-run facilities
- **[correct]** The water-sources limb applies: State maintenance and public dedication are both met

*Explanation:* The text reads 'wholly or partly' out of State funds, and public dedication is satisfied.

*Why:* Both conditions of the water-sources limb are met here. The first distractor turns on a single word in the text: partial State maintenance is expressly sufficient. A private farm pond would fail that limb and route elsewhere.

*Trap note:* Close-text trap. 'Wholly or partly' is the actual wording; an option demanding wholly is quoting the provision wrongly.

**Q4** _(round F / trap B)_  A State government order reserves clerk posts for candidates born within the district. What is the correct constitutional objection?

- [ ] Backward-class reservation cannot apply, district-born candidates not forming a class
- **[correct]** Residence qualifications for State posts may be prescribed only by Parliament, by law
- [ ] The five-ground clause is breached, place of birth being one of those listed grounds
- [ ] The special AP arrangement is breached, local preference requiring a Presidential Order

*Explanation:* The residence power sits with Parliament alone; a State executive order cannot exercise it.

*Why:* The five-ground clause does list place of birth, but it does not govern public employment at all, which is why the third option fails despite being half-true. The employment clause carries seven grounds, and its residence proviso reserves the power to Parliament.

*Trap note:* Two clauses both mention place of birth. Only one of them governs employment; identify the subject-matter before the ground.

**Q5** _(round F / trap D)_  A panchayat bars Dalit households from a village shrine festival. Which statement about the nature of the protection engaged is accurate?

- **[correct]** Absolute: no reasonable restriction, no Emergency suspension, and it binds private parties
- [ ] Absolute against the State, but private conduct is reached only through penal statutes
- [ ] Non-suspendable in an Emergency, but enforceable only against State instrumentalities
- [ ] Subject to reasonable restriction in the interest of public order, as other rights are

*Explanation:* The abolition admits no restriction and no suspension, and it reaches private conduct directly.

*Why:* This is the one equality guarantee with no reasonable-restriction clause and no Emergency carve-out, and it operates horizontally of its own force. Penal statutes enforce it; they are not the source of its reach against private parties.

*Trap note:* Three of the four options concede some limit. The guarantee that concedes none is the one being tested.

**Q6** _(round F / trap E)_  An officer is transferred to a lower-status post with no ground recorded, and the file shows that none existed. Which principle governs the challenge?

- [ ] Service transfers are policy matters, reviewable only for legislative competence
- [ ] Only the fairness of the procedure is in issue, the substance being non-justiciable
- **[correct]** Arbitrary State action breaches equality even where no classification is involved
- [ ] The classification test applies: the transfer must rest on a real, relevant difference

*Explanation:* No classification exists to examine; the defect is the absence of any determining principle.

*Why:* The classification test has nothing to bite on where a single individual is singled out with no criterion at all. That gap is precisely what the arbitrariness limb was developed to close, and it operates independently of the two-limb test.

*Trap note:* Where the stem contains no grouping at all, the classification option is a decoy. Look for the arbitrariness limb.

**Q7** _(round F / trap F)_  Match A-equality before law, B-the five listed grounds, C-equality of opportunity in employment, D-abolition of untouchability with P, Q, R and S in article order. Which code is correct?

- [ ] A-P  B-Q  C-S  D-R
- [ ] A-Q  B-P  C-R  D-S
- [ ] A-P  B-R  C-Q  D-S
- **[correct]** A-P  B-Q  C-R  D-S

*Explanation:* Article order runs equality before law, five grounds, employment, untouchability.

*Why:* Solve from the most distinctive item rather than left to right. The single-line abolition and the equality-before-law phrase are immovable; once both are fixed the remaining pair has only one arrangement.

*Trap note:* Anchor the two unmistakable pairs first. Match sets are solved from the unique item inwards.

**Q8** _(round F / trap I)_  A recruitment advertisement excludes applicants on grounds of descent and of residence. Which statement about those two grounds is accurate?

- [ ] Residence appears in both clauses; descent is confined to the employment clause
- **[correct]** Both grounds appear in the employment clause and in neither of the other guarantees
- [ ] Descent appears in the five-ground clause and residence only in the employment clause
- [ ] Both grounds appear in the five-ground clause, which also governs public employment

*Explanation:* Descent and residence are additions unique to the seven-ground employment clause.

*Why:* The five grounds are religion, race, caste, sex and place of birth. Descent and residence are the two additions that take the employment clause to seven. Options one and two are partial-truth traps, conceding one ground and misplacing the other.

*Trap note:* Either word in a stem routes the question to the employment clause and rules out the five-ground clause entirely.

**Q9** _(round F / trap C)_  An Indian citizen accepts a foreign knighthood without any clearance. Which statement of the position is correct?

- **[correct]** Barred outright: the prohibition on citizens accepting foreign titles admits no consent
- [ ] Permitted with the President's consent, which may be granted before or after acceptance
- [ ] Barred unless the honour is an award for excellence, which is not a title at all
- [ ] Permitted, professional and academic distinctions falling outside the prohibition

*Explanation:* The citizen bar is absolute. The consent route serves non-citizens holding office of profit.

*Why:* Two separate axes are in play. First, citizen versus non-citizen office-holder decides whether consent is even available. Second, award versus title decides whether the prohibition is engaged at all. A knighthood is a title, and the holder is a citizen.

*Trap note:* Consent never rescues a citizen. The consent mechanism belongs to a different clause and a different class of person.

**Q10** _(round F / trap G)_  A recruitment rule makes only left-handed applicants eligible for constable posts. What is the outcome under the classification test?

- [ ] Valid: recruitment standards are policy and attract only a competence review
- [ ] Fails: handedness is one of the grounds on which discrimination is expressly barred
- **[correct]** Fails: the difference is real but bears no rational nexus to the duties of the post
- [ ] Valid: a real and identifiable difference satisfies the classification requirement

*Explanation:* Limb one is satisfied and limb two is not; the limbs are cumulative.

*Why:* Handedness is genuinely identifiable, so the differentia exists. What is missing is any connection between handedness and constabulary duties. Clearing one limb never validates a classification.

*Trap note:* A real difference alone proves nothing. The nexus limb is where most such rules actually die.

**Q11** _(round C / trap I)_  A canteen refuses access on grounds of religion, and separately a post is denied on grounds of descent. The two wrongs are challenged together. Which routing is correct?

- [ ] Access denial to the employment clause; descent denial to the five-ground clause
- [ ] Both to the employment clause, which lists religion and descent alongside each other
- [ ] Both to the five-ground clause, which reaches employment through its access limb
- **[correct]** Access denial to the five-ground clause; descent denial to the employment clause

*Explanation:* Religion-based access routes generally; descent exists only in the employment clause.

*Why:* The employment clause does list religion as well as descent, which is what makes the second option tempting. But it governs employment alone and cannot absorb a canteen-access wrong. Paired wrongs usually need paired provisions.

*Trap note:* One article rarely covers two different wrongs. Route each limb of the stem separately before comparing options.

**Q12** _(round C / trap C)_  An unaided non-minority college denies an EWS seat; a minority institution denies a backward-class seat. Which statement of the position is correct?

- [ ] Both breach the private-institution quota clause, which covers all unaided colleges
- **[correct]** The first breaches the private-institution quota clause; the second is shielded by minority rights
- [ ] The first is shielded as an unaided body; the second breaches minority-institution duties
- [ ] Neither breaches: the quota clause is enabling only and creates no enforceable duty

*Explanation:* The quota clause covers unaided non-minority colleges but expressly excepts minority institutions.

*Why:* The carve-out exists to protect the right of a minority to establish AND administer its own institution. Both limbs are required before the shield operates; an institution merely administered by a minority does not qualify.

*Trap note:* The carve-out is express, not implied. Check both limbs of the minority right before applying the shield.

**Q13** _(round C / trap G)_  A law effectuating farm-welfare policy and a law effectuating resource-ownership policy are both challenged for inequality, each pleading the Directive-Principle shield. Which is shielded?

- **[correct]** Only the resource-ownership law; the shield reaches two Directive clauses alone
- [ ] Neither: the shield protects only against the trade-freedom guarantee, not equality
- [ ] Only the farm-welfare law, welfare being the declared purpose of the shield
- [ ] Both, the shield extending to any law effectuating a Directive Principle

*Explanation:* The wide shield was cut down in 1980; only the two resource clauses survive.

*Why:* The fourth option restates the version of the shield that was struck down, which is why it reads so plausibly. The shield covers challenges on both equality and freedom grounds, which disposes of the first option.

*Trap note:* A superseded provision quoted as current law is the commonest trap in this topic. Check the date of the version being described.

**Q14** _(round C / trap F)_  Place in correct sequence: the arbitrariness doctrine, the Emergency repair, the cutting down of the shield, the quota-method ruling, the EWS insertion, the EWS upholding.

- [ ] 1974 - 1980 - 1978 - 1992 - 2019 - 2022
- [ ] 1978 - 1974 - 1980 - 1992 - 2022 - 2019
- **[correct]** 1974 - 1978 - 1980 - 1992 - 2019 - 2022
- [ ] 1974 - 1978 - 1992 - 1980 - 2019 - 2022

*Explanation:* Each step answers the one before it; adjacent swaps are the trap.

*Why:* The repair follows the excess it corrected, the cutting-down follows the widening, the quota-method ruling follows the Mandal dispute, and an insertion necessarily precedes its upholding. Reason from what answered what rather than from memorised dates.

*Trap note:* Options differing by a single adjacent swap test whether you know the causal order, not the calendar.

**Q15** _(round C / trap G)_  A law capping the size of Councils of Ministers and a law inserting EWS reservation are to be matched to their amendments. Which pairing is correct?

- [ ] Ministerial cap to the 97th; EWS insertion to the 103rd

- [ ] Ministerial cap to the 103rd; EWS insertion to the 91st
- **[correct]** Ministerial cap to the 91st; EWS insertion to the 103rd

*Explanation:* Cap in 2003, EWS in 2019. The 97th is cooperatives and the 102nd is the backward-classes commission.

*Why:* All four numbers name real amendments, so recognition alone will not separate them. Anchor each to its year and its subject: 2003 cap, 2011 cooperatives, 2018 commission, 2019 EWS.

*Trap note:* Close-number sets defeat familiarity. Anchor by year and subject, never by the number alone.

**Q16** _(round C / trap G)_  Rule X is fair on its face but enforced against a single rival shop with no stated distinction; Rule Y authorises seizure with no hearing. Which doctrines govern them respectively?

- [ ] Both engage arbitrariness, the absence of a hearing being an arbitrary act in itself
- **[correct]** Selective enforcement engages arbitrariness; denial of a hearing engages fair procedure
- [ ] Selective enforcement engages fair procedure; denial of a hearing engages arbitrariness
- [ ] Both engage fair procedure, selective enforcement being a defect of process as well

*Explanation:* Valid law with void selection is arbitrariness; void procedure is the fairness requirement.

*Why:* Separate the law from its application before choosing a doctrine. Rule X is unimpeachable until it is applied; Rule Y is defective in its own terms. The two defects sit at different stages and attract different tests.

*Trap note:* Ask at which stage the defect arises: in the rule itself, or only in how it was applied.

**Q17** _(round C / trap B)_  A State Assembly enacts a residence requirement for State posts, and an AP zonal quota operates under a Presidential Order. What is the validity of each?

- **[correct]** The Assembly law fails; the zonal quota is valid under the special AP arrangement
- [ ] The Assembly law is valid; the zonal quota fails for want of a Parliamentary law
- [ ] Both fail: local-area preference in recruitment has no constitutional foundation
- [ ] Both are valid: residence conditions and local cadres rest on the same source of power

*Explanation:* Residence power is Parliament's by law; AP local cadres flow from a distinct special provision.

*Why:* The fourth option states the error the question is built on. The two arrangements look alike because both restrict recruitment geographically, but one requires a law of Parliament and the other a Presidential Order under a provision written for the State.

*Trap note:* Read for the ACTOR and the INSTRUMENT: Parliament by law, or the President by order. They are not interchangeable.

**Q18** _(round C / trap G)_  A proclamation is issued today on the ground of internal disturbance, and all freedoms are treated as automatically suspended. Which statement is correct?

*Trap note:* Where options form a two-by-two matrix, elimination fails. Settle each variable separately, then read off the pair.

**Q19** _(round C / trap I)_  A State-aided school denies admission to an applicant on grounds of caste; separately, a linguistic minority institution asserts a right to administer its own admissions. Which routing is correct?

**Q20** _(round F / trap B)_  A soldier disciplined for joining a political union challenges the action as a breach of the freedom of association. What is the outcome?

## CURRENT AFFAIRS / ANALYTICAL (10)

**Q1** _(round X / trap G)_  A law treating two groups differently survives challenge because the court finds a real difference between them bearing a link to the law's purpose. Which doctrine has been applied?

- **[correct]** The valid-classification test: a real difference bearing a nexus to the object
- [ ] The colourable-legislation test: indirect exercise of a power not directly held
- [ ] The pith-and-substance test: the true character of a law for competence purposes
- [ ] The basic-structure test: whether an amendment damages an essential feature

*Explanation:* Difference plus link to the object is the two-limb classification test.

*Why:* All four are real constitutional doctrines, each stated accurately, so only the fact-pattern separates them. Competence doctrines ask who may legislate; the classification test asks whether a distinction is permissible.

*Trap note:* Reverse-engineer from the reasoning described. 'Difference plus link' names one doctrine only.

**Q2** _(round X / trap J)_  Advice is given that since the five-ground clause covers citizens, a foreign company refused a licence has no equality remedy at all. What is the flaw in that advice?

- [ ] It omits the trade-freedom guarantee, available to foreign and Indian traders alike
- [ ] It is accurate: no equality guarantee in Part III is available to a foreign company

*Explanation:* The citizen limitation is correctly stated but fatally incomplete.

*Why:* The advice is ninety per cent right, which is what makes it dangerous. The two rejected additions are themselves citizens-only, so they could not cure the gap; only the person-based guarantee does.

*Trap note:* Advice that states a true rule may still be wrong for what it leaves out. Ask what qualification is missing.

**Q3** _(round X / trap I)_  A detenu has been held four months with no Advisory Board reference, though grounds were communicated and a representation was permitted. Which requirement decides the outcome?

*Explanation:* The specific review track governs; the satisfied requirements cannot save the detention.

*Why:* The second option imports the punitive-arrest timetable into a preventive case, where it does not apply at all. Where a general and a specific provision both appear to fit, the specific one governs.

*Trap note:* Preventive detention runs on its own track. Production before a magistrate belongs to the other one.

*Trap note:* Trace fact to provision to statute to duty to consequence. A broken link changes the answer entirely.
